Design flags

    Legal

    In the United States a suppressor is regulated under the National Firearms Act. Making one requires an approved ATF Form 1 before any manufacturing begins, including parts and "solvent trap" kits intended for conversion, plus the required engraving. Several states prohibit civilian ownership entirely, and most other countries have their own rules. This page is a conceptual teaching aid. It produces no machining drawings or print files.

    Method, sources and limits
    • Muzzle pressure comes from approximate published reference values for each cartridge, scaled with barrel length as P ∝ L-1.3 and capped at 60 % of the SAAMI chamber limit. Real values depend heavily on the powder, bullet and barrel.
    • Blast chamber pressure treats the gas column (case capacity + bore volume) at muzzle pressure as expanding isentropically into the blast chamber: Pbc = Pm·(Vgas/(Vgas+Vbc))1.25. This quasi-static estimate is why a bigger blast chamber lowers the load on the wall.
    • Design wall pressure = Pbc × dynamic load factor. The DLF covers the impulsive, reflected-shock part of the load that the quasi-static estimate misses. Raise it for short barrels, sealed designs and full-auto use.
    • Wall check uses the Lamé thick-wall solution for peak hoop stress at the bore of the tube: σ = P(ro² + ri²)/(ro² − ri²). The allowable stress is yield × temperature knock-down × process factor ÷ safety factor.
    • Not modelled: baffle strikes, erosion, fatigue from cycling, thread and end-cap shear, notch effects, porosity in printed parts, or the extra load when a bullet is off-axis. Real products are proven by proof-firing and destructive testing, not by this formula.
    • Industry practice, such as stacked-baffle cans, welded or integral stacks, flow-through printed Inconel and titanium designs, and monocore rimfire and pistol cans, is described here only in general terms that are publicly known. The material values are typical handbook numbers, not any manufacturer's specification.
    • PEW Science publishes independent suppressor test reports: muzzle and at-ear sound pressure, the Suppressor Sound Rating, first-round pop, back pressure and gas flow. This page does not reproduce their proprietary data. The scores follow the general trends their reports discuss. For real numbers, read their reports.
    • Open-source community 3D-printed designs are described only as a category. No files from them are included or linked.
